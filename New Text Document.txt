# Sub-Volt TEG Energy Harvesting Boost Converter (BQ25504)

This repository contains the KiCad 8.0 hardware design, TINA-TI transient simulations, and project documentation for a sub-volt thermoelectric generator (TEG) energy harvesting boost converter. 

Designed around the Texas Instruments **BQ25504** ultra-low-power harvester, this circuit steps up $300\text{mV}$–$600\text{mV}$ inputs to a regulated $3.3\text{V}$ to intermittently power an ESP32-C3 telemetry load from a $10\text{mF}$ supercapacitor.

## ⚡ System Architecture & Specs
* **Harvester IC:** TI BQ25504 (330 nA quiescent current)
* **Cold-Start Voltage:** 330 mV (steps up from zero without external battery)
* **MPPT Network:** 50% Fractional Open-Circuit Voltage tracking for TEG source (sampled every 16s)
* **Storage Element:** 10 mF radial supercapacitor (provides 33.64 mJ per discharge cycle)
* **Voltage Thresholds (Custom Megaohm Dividers):**
  * Overvoltage Cutoff ($V_{\text{OV}}$): ~3.30 V
  * Undervoltage Cutoff ($V_{\text{UV}}$): ~2.04 V

## 🛠️ Hardware Design (sikhi_v1)
The 2-layer FR4 PCB (33.35 x 25.80 mm) was designed in KiCad 8.0 with strict analog layout rules:
* **Switching Node Isolation:** The noisy $22\ \mu\text{H}$ inductor switching loop is physically partitioned away from the high-impedance $10\text{ M}\Omega$ MPPT and threshold sensing dividers.
* **Thermal Grounding:** The BQ25504 VQFN-16 exposed pad is stitched to an unbroken bottom ground plane using four $0.20\text{ mm}$ thermal vias.
* **High-Frequency Bypassing:** A $100\text{ nF}$ low-ESL ceramic capacitor is placed immediately adjacent to the `VSTOR` pin to shunt MHz switching harmonics.
